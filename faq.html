Water & Drying

About the drying process

Most residential structural drying runs three to five days. Heavily saturated subfloor, hardwood or multi-room losses can take longer. We take daily moisture readings and only pull the equipment once those readings confirm the structure is genuinely dry.

Household fans move surface air but don't pull moisture out of drywall, subfloor and framing. Water that looks dry on the surface can stay trapped in the wall cavity — and that hidden moisture is exactly where mold starts. Commercial air movers paired with dehumidifiers and actual moisture measurement are what finish the job.

Usually no. Most water losses can be dried with you still living there, though the equipment is noisy and the affected rooms will be off limits. Often, yes — especially if we reach it quickly and the water was clean. Carpet can frequently be dried in place with the pad replaced, and drywall can often be dried without removal. Mold & Air Quality

Mold questions

Yes. Outdoor humidity has little to do with it. A slow leak under a sink or inside a wall creates a dark, damp cavity that stays humid regardless of how dry it is outside. We find mold in Valley homes regularly — usually from a leak nobody knew about.

Given moisture and organic material, growth can begin within roughly 24 to 48 hours. That window is the single biggest reason we push for fast extraction and professional drying rather than waiting to see if it dries on its own.

Bleach may lighten surface staining, but it doesn't address growth inside porous material and it doesn't fix the moisture source feeding it. Without containment, scrubbing can also spread spores into the rest of the house. Proper remediation means isolating the area, removing affected material and correcting the leak.
